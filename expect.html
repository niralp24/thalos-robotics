What to Expect

Come curious.
Leave capable.

You do not need to arrive knowing how to build an underwater robot. You do need to be ready to learn, contribute, communicate, and keep showing up.

01

Your first meetings

Start with the whole system.

New members are introduced to the competition, team workflow, safety expectations, and the relationship between mechanical, electrical, software, documentation, and piloting.

02

Choose a starting area

You can begin with the subteam that best matches your interests, then explore other areas as the season develops.

03

Learn by doing

Expect short lessons followed by practical work: measuring, modeling, wiring, coding, documenting, testing, or practicing operations.

04

Work in iterations

Not every idea will work. Members are expected to record results, ask for help, and improve the next version.

A typical team cycle

Plan. Build. Test. Review.

Meeting formats change throughout the season, but the work follows a consistent engineering rhythm.

1Set the goal

Define the task and what success looks like.

2Do the work

Build, code, wire, write, or practice.

3Test it

Collect evidence instead of relying on assumptions.

4Review together

Share progress, blockers, and next steps.

Commitment

Consistency matters more than experience.

Exact meeting schedules will be shared with accepted members. As competition approaches, the team may add build sessions, pool testing, documentation work, and practice presentations.

Show up prepared

Communicate early when you cannot attend or complete an assigned task.

Work safely

Follow tool, electrical, water, and competition safety procedures.

Document your work

Record decisions, test results, failures, and changes so the whole team can learn.

Respect every role

Builders, programmers, writers, organizers, pilots, and presenters all affect performance.

Frequently asked questions

Before you apply.

Do I need robotics experience?

Prior robotics experience is preferred, but it is not the only way to contribute. A foundation in electronics, engineering, programming, design, writing, media, or organization can also be valuable.

Do I have to attend a specific school?

No. Thalos Robotics is independent and open to high school students across the Silicon Valley region.

Can I work on more than one subteam?

Yes. Members may begin with one primary area while learning how their work connects to other parts of the system.

Will everyone pilot the ROV?

Pilot and co-pilot roles require practice and coordination. Interested members can train, but competition roles are assigned based on preparation and team needs.

What should I bring to the first meeting?

Bring curiosity, a way to take notes, and any questions you have. Required tools, software, or safety equipment will be explained before use.
