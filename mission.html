What we do

Real missions.
Real engineering.

The MATE ROV Competition challenges teams to design and pilot underwater robots to complete real-world environmental missions. Each task requires genuine engineering: from gripper mechanisms to sensor integration to buoyancy-controlled floats.

For Thalos, each mission becomes a systems problem: understand the rules, identify the physical challenge, prototype a solution, integrate it with the vehicle, and test it until the whole team can operate it consistently.

01

Coral Collection

Retrieve with control.

Navigate the ROV to the pool floor and retrieve specimen objects using a custom-built gripper arm.

  • Manipulator geometry and grip force
  • Camera placement and pilot visibility
  • Buoyancy changes while carrying an object
  • Fast, repeatable operator procedure
02

Sensor Deployment

Place with precision.

Carry and precisely place a simulated instrument package at an underwater target location.

  • Stable payload transport
  • Accurate alignment underwater
  • Release mechanism reliability
  • Team communication during placement
03

Infrastructure Install

Work like an offshore system.

Simulate offshore energy infrastructure tasks using the ROV's manipulator systems.

  • Tool access around the frame
  • Torque, leverage, and mechanical limits
  • Controlled approach to a target
  • Protection of wiring and tether
04

MATE Floats

Control depth on purpose.

Deploy a buoyancy-controlled float that ascends and descends to specific depths autonomously or on command.

  • Buoyancy and ballast calculations
  • Depth sensing and control logic
  • Waterproof electronics packaging
  • Repeatable testing and calibration
